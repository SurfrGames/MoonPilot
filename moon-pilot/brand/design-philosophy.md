# Ballistic Calm

A visual language for things that move under one law and nothing else. Every form is the
trace of a force: a body, the curve it bends, and the small bright thing that rides the curve.
Nothing is decorated. A line exists because something travelled it.

**Space and form.** The canvas is mostly dark and mostly empty, because the subject is mostly
distance. One large quiet mass anchors the composition; one tilted ellipse passes behind it and
in front of it, so depth is stated with a single overlap rather than perspective tricks. Shapes
are perfect primitives: disc, ellipse, wedge. Each edge is placed with painstaking attention, the
way an instrument maker places a graduation mark.

**Color and material.** Three temperatures only. Cold regolith grey for what is massive and
still. A warm amber for what burns and is chosen. A thin cold cyan for what follows without
being alive. Grey never glows, amber never fills a large area, cyan is never the brightest thing
present. The restraint is the craft; the palette should look like it was calibrated over many
evenings, not picked.

**Scale and rhythm.** The mass is large, the traveller is tiny, and the path between them is the
longest element. A trail thickens and brightens toward its head so that direction and time are
read from weight alone. Repetition is slight: the same curve, drawn once boldly and once as a
whisper, the second always a little behind the first.

**Type.** Sparse, wide-tracked capitals with open counters, set like the legend on a chart.
A light weight for the place, a firmer weight for the person. Monospaced annotations in the
margins are small and clinical: figure numbers, sizes, constants. Words label what the drawing
has already said and never explain it.

**Craft.** The work must read as meticulously made by someone at the top of their field:
supersampled edges, optical alignment, generous margins, nothing touching that should not
touch. It has to hold at sixteen pixels and at a metre wide. If an element cannot survive being
tiny, it is removed rather than rescued. The finished mark should feel inevitable, the product
of countless small refusals.
